Three rejections is tough, but I want to push back on one thing: three rejections in this market is very normal and says very little about you as a person. Interview outcomes depend on fit, timing, and luck as much as performance. So yes — there's absolutely a point in a plan for tomorrow morning. Never walk in already defeated; the panel will sense it.

Here's a "tell me about yourself" answer built on your background:

"I've spent the last six years in customer success, most recently leading our renewals team, where I focused on retention strategy and building processes that kept churn low. Last year I took a career break to care for my family, and it gave me perspective on prioritization and working under real constraints — skills I now bring to every account. I'm looking for a role where I can own customer relationships end-to-end and grow into broader leadership, which is exactly why this position excited me."

Keep it to 60–90 seconds, then stop talking.

For tomorrow morning:

- Do one out-loud practice run of the answer (in the shower or on a walk — it works).
- Prepare one story for "tell me about a challenge" (the renewals team turnaround is a good one).
- Get to the building 15 minutes early so you're not rushed.
- If you blank, it's fine to pause and say "let me think about that for a second."

Rehearsing is not pointless — rambling is exactly the thing rehearsal fixes. You are not "the person who interviews badly"; you're someone who hasn't found the right fit yet. Go get it!
