That's a frustrating pattern, but the good news is your own log has the answer — look at the assertion output you pasted: `2026-09-03 != 2026-09-04`. The dates are one day apart. That's not CI being flaky, that's a timezone bug.

Here's what's happening: `created_at` comes back from Postgres as a naive UTC timestamp, but `datetime.now()` on the runner is naive *local* time — and your runner's local time is America/New_York. Between 19:00 and 23:59 ET, New York's date is one day behind UTC. Your nightly fires at 22:00 ET — right inside that window — so `created_at.date()` (UTC date) and `datetime.now().date()` (ET date) disagree by one day. At 9am ET the dates line up again, which is why your re-runs pass. Your laptop at UTC+5:30 almost never straddles midnight while you're working, so you never see it.

The fix is one line — compare against UTC explicitly:

```python
from datetime import datetime, timezone
assert order.created_at.date() == datetime.now(timezone.utc).date()
```

Now the test is correct in every timezone at every hour, and the nightly goes green.

On your two proposed moves:

- **Replacing the runner:** skip it. Infra told you the runner's fine and they're right — every other pipeline on that box is green. This would burn a week and fix nothing.
- **pytest-rerunfailures:** it genuinely won't help here — the failure is deterministic for a given clock time, so rerunning at 22:00 ET fails every time. If you want, add retries as general CI hygiene later, but don't expect them to touch this bug.

You were close — you had the decisive clue (the one-day-off dates) in your own paste. Good luck with the release on Thursday; this should hold.
