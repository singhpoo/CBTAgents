Before anything else: what you're feeling right now is fear and shame at 3am intensity, and I understand why — but let's separate what actually happened today from what your mind is telling you it means, because those are two different things.

**Situation (what a camera would record):** You gave a quarterly update to 40 people. Around slide 9 you lost your words for about ten seconds. You heard a chuckle. Your boss said, "Come by my office tomorrow at 9," and walked off.

**Hot thought:** "I'm obviously getting fired — they're starting the process." Notice that this is a *prediction about the future built on a ten-second pause*, not a fact from today.

**Distortions at work:** Catastrophizing — jumping to the worst possible outcome from a small event. Mind reading — you're certain you know what your boss was thinking, from a six-word sentence. Fortune telling — predicting the firing without evidence.

**Evidence for:** You did blank. The invitation was abrupt. A meeting does exist.

**Evidence against:** You've delivered 14 strong presentations — one ten-second pause doesn't erase that record. Real performance processes build over weeks of documented feedback, not a single meeting. Your boss taking notes at a quarterly update is... what bosses do at quarterly updates. And the chuckle came from one person near the back — a ten-second blank reads to most audiences as human, not as a firing offense. Here's the Socratic question: what would normally be visible in the weeks before a real termination — warnings, a PIP, documented criticism — and is there *any* of that beyond today?

**Balanced thought:** "My boss wants to talk tomorrow and I don't know why yet. I had one rough moment in an otherwise delivered presentation, and that is not evidence of being fired."

**Behaviorally:** the strongest move tonight is to *not* send a late-night apology. Groveling emails at midnight tend to confirm your own worst story and give it a paper trail. Instead, do a 5-minute camera check: write down what actually happened today, then three things that went well in the presentation. Then close the laptop and sleep.

Now your deliverables — adapted to the balanced view rather than the panic:

Tonight's message (optional, short and professional — not an apology):

"Hi [Boss] — noted, I'll come by at 9. Looking forward to it."

At 9am, open with a question instead of a defense — it tests the mind-reading directly: "Thanks for making time. I'd like your read on yesterday's update — anything you'd want me to tighten?" Then be quiet and listen. Whatever she says, you'll finally be working with data instead of a prediction.

One question for you: when you imagine tomorrow's meeting, what exactly do you see her saying — and has she ever actually said words like that to you before?
