Two days of silence from your best friend, right after the biggest news of your life — that genuinely hurts, and I'm not going to pretend it doesn't. Let's hold two things at once: the hurt is real, and the story attached to it is not yet verified.

**Situation (what a camera would record):** Tuesday you called; no answer. You sent a long, excited text. It was read at 6:41pm. Since then: no reply, two days. She was online and posted a story.

**Hot thought:** "She's obviously jealous, and this is her pulling away for good."

**Distortions at work:** Mind reading — you're diagnosing her inner state (jealousy) from a read receipt. Fortune telling — "for good" predicts the end of a three-year friendship from a 48-hour silence. All-or-nothing thinking — the options have collapsed to 'best friend as always' or 'over forever', with no middle where life is just busy.

**Evidence for:** The silence is genuinely unusual for someone close to you — that discrepancy is real and worth checking.

**Evidence against:** Three years of responsiveness is a lot of data pointing the other way. 'Online' and 'posted a story' say nothing about whether she's read your message, let alone how she felt. And there's a whole range of innocent explanations your story skips: an overwhelming week, something hard happening in *her* life (sometimes people go quiet when they're struggling with exactly the thing a friend just achieved), or simply not knowing what to say and letting it sit. So try this one: if a friend told you this exact story, what are all the explanations you'd list before 'she's jealous of me'? Why does your own case skip that list?

**Balanced thought:** "Two days of silence is unusual for Maya and worth checking — and it is not yet evidence of jealousy or the end of anything."

**Behaviorally:** here's the concrete experiment — put the phone down without sending anything tonight, and set yourself a rule: if there's no reply by Thursday evening, send one direct, warm message. That's it. The snarky draft is a protest behavior — it's designed to make the hurt visible, and it will start the very fight it's trying to prevent.

Now your deliverable. The revised text keeps your honesty, drops the barbs:

"Hey — I haven't heard from you since Tuesday, and I miss you. No pressure to reply tonight; I just want to check we're good. Also: I got into the masters program. Three years of wanting this — I keep grinning at random. Call me when you can? 💛"

And the decision: **wait, don't send tonight** — not as a game, but because a message sent to be *seen* at midnight tends to carry charge. Tomorrow, rested, you can send it as a gift rather than a test.

One question for you: when Maya went quiet, your mind said 'jealousy.' What's the kindest explanation you can think of — and how would you want *her* to interpret a two-day silence from you?
