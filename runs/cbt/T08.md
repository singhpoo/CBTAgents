One post, 200 comments, and an entire self-assessment has been rebuilt around it — let's inspect that building before you move into it.

**Situation (what a camera would record):** Your roommate, same graduating year, was promoted to Director of Engineering — her fourth promotion. You are 29, four years at your company, one raise, no title change. That is the complete factual inventory. Notice what isn't in it: data about "everyone from college," about being "10x behind," or about what the next ten months of your life are worth.

**Hot thought:** "I'm officially behind at life — the cautionary tale of our class."

**Distortions at work:** Mental filter — one LinkedIn post has seized the whole screen, and the invisible background (what you've built, learned, survived in four years) has been filtered out of *your* story too. Labeling — "behind at life" treats a person as a position on a race track. Should statements — "I should be 10x further along" is a rule invented after seeing someone else's scoreboard. Overgeneralization — "everyone is ahead of me" from one data point.

**Evidence for:** There is a real, specific gap worth noticing — she has a title you don't have yet. That's not imagined, and dismissing it entirely wouldn't help you either.

**Evidence against:** A promotion post is a highlight, not a biography — it tells you nothing about her trade-offs, luck, or hours, and nothing about the 200 people who didn't comment. "Behind" requires a fixed finish line that careers don't have; there is no officially sanctioned age for Director. And the comparison is running on two different datasets: her outside, you inside — you know every one of your own doubts and none of hers. So here's the question I'd press on: strip her post out of the story entirely — if nothing had been published today, what would you want the next twelve months to be *for*?

**Balanced thought:** "Her post is one data point about her career, not a measurement of mine. There's a real question here — do I want more growth, and what kind? — and it deserves an answer built on my values, not on a feed."

**Behaviorally:** two concrete moves. First, stimulus control: mute her (and the three accounts that trigger this) for 30 days — not forever, just while you rebuild the comparison's data set; every daily exposure re-runs the distortion. Second, tonight, 10 minutes, pen and paper: write your own two-column highlight post — what you've actually done in four years, and what you'd want to be true at 30 if no one was watching. That page, not her post, becomes the specification for the plan.
