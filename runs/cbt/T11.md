**Evidence for the hot thought:** The failure is real, recurring, and only in one environment. That's genuinely maddening, and your suspicion of the runner wasn't unreasonable two weeks ago.

**Evidence against:** A broken runner fails many pipelines, not one test at one hour. The failure is *precisely scheduled* — 22:00 ET, not "sometimes." And the assertion itself hands you the mechanism: the two dates differ by exactly one day. Between 19:00 and 23:59 ET, New York's local date lags UTC by one day. **Balanced thought:** "This isn't a broken CI — it's a timezone bug with a schedule. My test compares a naive UTC date from Postgres against `datetime.now().date()`, which is naive *local* time on the runner. The 22:00 ET nightly sits inside the window where ET is one day behind UTC; the 9am re-run and my laptop sit outside it."

**The experiment (30 seconds, before you patch anything):** on the runner or any machine, run `TZ=America/New_York python3 -c "from datetime import datetime; print(datetime.now().date())"` at different simulated times — or simply print both sides of the assertion in the test. **The fix (the real one):**

```python
assert order.created_at.date() == datetime.now(timezone.utc).date()
```

UTC against UTC — green at every hour, in every timezone.
