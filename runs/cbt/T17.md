Two days lost to a permission error and a rewrite ticket waiting to be written — before we commit three sprints to that ticket, let's separate what the two days actually proved from what they seem to prove, because the ticket's premise depends on that difference.

**Situation (what a camera would record):** One library, wired into your service once. Two runs, same setup, same error: `PdfRenderError: failed to initialize font cache (permission denied)`. That is the complete evidence base. Notice what isn't in it: a failing render engine, a second environment, or a version check.

**Hot thought:** "The library is clearly broken on our stack." It feels settled because two days feel like a thorough trial — but it's one error, observed twice, in one configuration.

**Distortions at work:** Overgeneralization — from one font-cache permission error to "the library is broken," a verdict about the whole tool. All-or-nothing thinking — the options have collapsed to "works as wired" or "build our own," with no middle where a missing system package is the whole story. And a dash of emotional reasoning — two days of frustration are doing the concluding.

**Evidence for the hot thought:** The error is real, reproducible in your setup, and font handling in PDF libs genuinely is a known pain point — your instinct isn't invented.

**Evidence against:** A *permission denied* on a font cache is an environment error, not an engine failure — it says the library couldn't find or write a cache, which in containers usually means the image has no font packages (`fonts-liberation`, `fontconfig`) or the cache dir isn't writable by the app user. Also unexamined: your pinned version versus latest. Here's the Socratic question that matters: what would "broken on our stack" require you to show — and does one environment error in one image come close to showing it?

**Balanced thought:** "One reproducible environment error, twice, in one container. The engine is untested; the font setup is the prime suspect; the rewrite decision is being made on evidence that would not survive a design review."

**Behavioral experiment (30 minutes, before any ticket):** run the minimal isolated repro outside your service —

```dockerfile
FROM python:3.12-slim
RUN apt-get update && apt-get install -y fonts-liberation fontconfig
RUN pip install pdfstack
```

then render a one-line invoice PDF. The experiment discriminates cleanly: PDF produced → the image was the bug, the fix is two Dockerfile lines, the rewrite is moot. Same error with fonts present and a writable cache dir → now you have a real repro, and the rewrite conversation deserves to happen — with evidence attached instead of frustration.
