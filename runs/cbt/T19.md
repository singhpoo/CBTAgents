A migration ticket with clean facts, a flat tone, and no doom in sight — refreshingly rare. Since there's no distress or interpretation to separate from fact here, I'll keep the psychology to one observation and spend the response where your actual question lives.

The one thought worth naming before Thursday: migrations like this carry a quiet assumption — *"it's a mechanical port, cron said it, beat will say it."* That's a should-statement doing invisible work; it's exactly the assumption that makes teams skip the three checks below. No anxiety detected or needed — just don't let the port's apparent mechanicalness talk you out of pinning the timezone.

**The mapping:**

```python
from celery.schedules import crontab

beat_schedule = {
    "sync-inventory":    {"task": "app.jobs.sync_inventory",    "schedule": crontab(minute="*/15")},
    "nightly-reconcile": {"task": "app.jobs.nightly_reconcile", "schedule": crontab(hour=3, minute=0, day_of_week="1-5")},
    "monthly-close":     {"task": "app.jobs.monthly_close",     "schedule": crontab(hour=4, minute=0, day_of_month=1)},
    # @reboot: no beat equivalent — see note 1
    "db-backup":         {"task": "app.jobs.db_backup",         "schedule": crontab(hour=2, minute=30, day_of_week=0)},
    "purge-sessions":    {"task": "app.jobs.purge_sessions",    "schedule": crontab(minute=0, hour="*/4")},
}
```

**1. `@reboot` doesn't convert.** Beat schedules are time-based, not lifecycle-based. Run `cleanup_tmp.sh` from your worker startup signal (`worker_ready`) or the service's init, or leave it in system cron — it's a system-hygiene job and cron is a fine home for it.

**2. Timezone semantics change.** System cron fired in the server's local time; beat fires in `CELERY_TIMEZONE` (UTC unless configured). Your 03:00 reconcile and 02:30 Sunday backup will shift silently if the worker's TZ differs from cron's was. Set `timezone` explicitly in the beat config and treat it as part of the migration, not a detail.

**3. Overlap on sync_inventory.** Every-15-minutes assumes the run fits in 15 minutes. On one cron box, overlap was mostly self-limiting; with a worker pool, a slow run stacks onto the next — double syncs, dirty inventory. Add a single-instance guard (`celery-once`, or a Redis `SET lock EX 840 NX` at task start).

**4. Sequencing changes with a shared worker.** The 4-hourly purge and the monthly close can now land on the same worker at the same time (both at 04:00 on the 1st, for instance) — a long close delays the purge and vice versa. If either is heavy, give it its own queue.

Everything else is a faithful 1:1 port. If sync_inventory is fast and idempotent, Thursday is low-risk — the checklist above is the difference between "it's just a port" being true and being the thing that pages you.
